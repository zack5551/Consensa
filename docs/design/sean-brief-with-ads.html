02

Ad research: what this layout earns

In the middle case, ads earn about CAD 2.50 a year per monthly user, within a range of CAD 1.70 to 4.30. That's CAD 1.7M to 4.3M a year at 1M users. The display slots alone cover about CAD 1.70 to 1.90. An Explore feed (more pages per visit) and sponsored question sets make up the rest. Canadian display rates are lower than in the US, which is why data licensing is the real upside.

Presets:

Good fit
  • Telecom and internet providers
  • Banks, credit unions and insurers (everyday products, not trading)
  • Retail, outdoor and seasonal brands
  • Travel, airlines and auto
  • Streaming, media and events
  • Restaurants and grocery
  • Government and public-service campaigns
Allowed with care
  • Alcohol: only to signed-in adults, geotargeted to each province's drinking age (18 or 19), following the CRTC code that BC applies online (Lexpert)
  • Sponsored question sets: the brand presents the set, but the questions are neutral and the brand never decides the result
  • Every ad in French as well as English for Quebec (Bill 96)
Off-limits
  • Sportsbooks, casinos and lotteries, with no affiliate links (LEX's rule for the free phase, and the federal Senate bill S-211 proposes a national ban on sports-betting ads)
  • Prediction markets and event-contract brokers
  • Crypto trading and payday loans
  • Cannabis (the Cannabis Act tightly restricts promotion)
  • Political and election ads
  • Anything aimed at minors

Why this matters for the bigger picture: keeping gambling ads out entirely is what keeps the "this isn't gambling" story clean, and it's also what makes the audience safe to sell to banks, telecoms and government.

The curiosity is there, but the appetite for wagering isn't. A free forecasting app reaches the majority that real-money platforms can't.

07

What it could be worth

On audience alone, 1M Canadian users points to about CAD 10M to 40M. There are three ways past that: grow to 3M to 4M users, build a real data business, or attract a strategic buyer who pays for the licensed path.

Value per monthly user at sale

The scale is logarithmic. Morning Consult (USD 1B+) and Lucid (USD 1.1B, about 9x revenue) show that opinion data alone can build billion-dollar businesses.

The premium precedent: theScore

A free Canadian sports app that grew to about 4M monthly users, added betting through a licence, and sold to PENN for about USD 2B in 2021. That's the "free audience plus a licensed path" case.

After 1M: the best route

Partner with, or sell to, a licensed operator or a provincial lottery. They run a separate real-money product, and we supply the audience, the tech and the data. Free points never carry over.

08

The legal line

Play money only, because that's where the law is clear. Canada's securities regulators limit real-money event contracts to economic, financial and climate topics (CSA/CIRO Staff Notice 91-307, Aug 27, 2026, and CIRO's Oct 16 update), and they leave sports and entertainment to provincial gaming law.

The free app always…

  • Shows probabilities and forecasts ("62% chance")
  • Is 18+, with separate consent for data use, built to Quebec's Law 25
  • Sells data only as anonymized aggregates
  • Uses AI to explain why a forecast moved

The free app never…

  • Lets points be bought, sold, cashed out or transferred
  • Uses odds or betting language
  • Lets the AI give picks or tips
  • Promises real money, or runs prizes without a skill-testing element

A lawyer will review this before launch. A free 30-minute Law Society referral consult is the first step.
